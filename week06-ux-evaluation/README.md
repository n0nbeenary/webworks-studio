# Week 6 — UX Evaluation Brief
## Hill Country Trail Guide

**Primary User:** Maya Torres  
**Primary Task:** Choose a beginner-appropriate Saturday hike that can be completed in about three hours or less.

---

## 1. Task Walkthrough
Briefly describe what Maya would try to do first, what she would look for, and where she might hesitate.

Maya would open the website on mobile and begin by trying to filter the trails to "Easy" but would be met with the friction point since the filter buttons do not work. She would scroll down to see the list of popular trails and click on "More info" on the Juniper Creek Loop card. She would see a wall of text with high cognitive load but the paragraph doesn't include the completion time for the trail so she would try to look for more information. She would scroll down the page more to glance over the information for the other two popular trails listed, but all the information is visually consistent and lacks clear indicators for her primary task, so she would get frustrated and close the website.

---

## 2. Five UX Findings

Document **exactly five meaningful findings**.

### Finding 1
**Observation:**  There is no primary CTA on the page.
**Evidence:**  There is no interactive element in the hero.
**User Impact:**  Maya will become confused and unsure how to naviagate the website.
**Principle:**  Navigation
**Priority:** Medium
**Recommendation:**  Add a CTA button in the hero that directs the user to a page with information for all the trails so user can recognize the next step.

### Finding 2
**Observation:**  The catergory buttons to filter the trails are not interactive
**Evidence:**  Buttons above "Choose a category above to narrow the list." are not interactive.
**User Impact:**  Maya will be confused on how to filter trails to her needs because the buttons do not match the instructions underneath them.
**Principle:**  Interaction Clarity
**Priority:**  High
**Recommendation:**  Remove this section from the homepage and add it to the Trails page. Change the buttons to a drop down menu and add a script to apply the appropriate filters as chosen by the user so the interaction matches their expectation.

### Finding 3
**Observation:**  The trail cards lack sufficient information to be effective summaries of each trail.
**Evidence:**  Each card lists two numbers, one related to miles and another related to feet however neither are explained. Estimated completion time is also not listed.
**User Impact:**  While Maya will be able to tell what level the trail is considered, she will not be able to determine how the other information would affect her planned hike.
**Principle:**  Clarity
**Priority:**  Medium
**Recommendation:**  Update each card to clarify what each number represents and add estimated completion time to provider user with important information efficiently.

### Finding 4
**Observation:**  The information for each trail does not emphasis key information, such as difficulty, distance, dog friendly status, or any associated fees. Each trail seems to focus on different information.
**Evidence:**  The paragraph for each trail is visually consistent and there is not emphasis for any important information beside the trail's name. Only Juniper Creek Loop lists Trail classification explicitly.
**User Impact:**  Maya is likely to glance over the information and miss the answers to her questions. She may understand the information listed under Juniper Creek Loop, but will not know where to find the same information for the other trails, that leading to frustration and confusion.
**Principle:**  Hierarchy + Consistency
**Priority:**  High
**Recommendation:**  Move the trail information to the Trail page and use different text styling to emphasis key information, such as difficulty, distance, estimated completion time, dog friendly status, and any associated fees, for each trail to uniformly provide information on each trail.

### Finding 5
**Observation:**  The "Plan your hike" button links back to the top of the homepage.
**Evidence:**  When the button at the bottom of the homepage is clicked, the user is taken back to the top of the homepage.
**User Impact:**  Maya will be confused why she is being shown the same information she just scrolled past and will bounce from the website.
**Principle:**  Navigation
**Priority:**  Medium
**Recommendation:**  Update the the link to the Tips page.

## 3. Top Three Priorities
Identify the three findings that should move forward into Week 7.

1. Remove the filter buttons from the homepage and add it to the Trails page. Change the buttons to a drop down menu and add a script to apply the appropriate filters as chosen by the user so the interaction matches their expectation. This will help Maya by making it so she can efficiently search for a trail that matches her needs.
2. Move the trail information to the Trail page and use different text styling to emphasis key information under each trail, such as difficulty, distance, estimated completion time, dog friendly status, and any associated fees, for each trail to uniformly provide information on each trail. This will help Maya by reducing the dognitive load associated with the homepage and make comparing information easier since she will know exactly where to look under each trail.
3. Update each trail card to clarify what each number represents and add estimated completion time to provider user with important information efficiently. Similar to prioritiy 2, this will allow Maya to find the answers to her questions efficiently.

For each, briefly explain why it matters to Maya's primary task.
